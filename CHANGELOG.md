# Changelog

## 0.1.0.0 — 2026-10-18

- First release in the Farm to Market 0.1 version series; continues the earlier 1.4.0.1-dev builds.
- Release package renamed to `FS25_RealisticLivestockF2M.zip`. Back up saves and remove the former `FS25_RealisticLivestockRM.zip` before updating; old mod-ID compatibility needs in-game verification.
- Includes the integrated livestock nutrition, body condition and economic changes, upstream 1.4.1.0 integration, Hof Bergmann animal-model fixes, species navigation, monthly input/output display and scrollable pen requirements.
- Replaces map-version startup warnings with a one-time invitation to report feedback on GitHub Issues.
- Features still disabled by default retain their existing switches and validation limits described below.

- T02 contract review fixes: recheck source ownership/queues across callbacks, verify the live target again before commit/compensation, refuse stale capacity, and certify production-cost receipts rather than refunding an interrupted money call from its balance alone. Added 46 regressions; transactions remain OFF by default and game validation is pending.

## Unreleased

- Added Settings > Player guide with chapter navigation, scrolling, and Chinese/English handbooks. Chinese game languages show Chinese; every other game language shows English. Chinese DDS text atlases avoid missing native glyphs; English uses native text without extra atlases. The guide identifies its dev.8 code baseline and separates optional and disabled systems from default gameplay.

- Each hour a pen's actual consumption (trough removals during the feeding and what the meadow lost; food added by a player or a feeding robot does not count) is shared between its animals by their food request: each gets its request's share of every liter eaten, valued at the fill type's energy and the breed's grass, hay and silage traits. What it eats is measured against what an average animal of its kind, age and state requests on full feed, so an animal that eats more liters (a high-yield cow, a bigger frame) gets more energy, one that eats fewer or poorer liters less. The budget pays maintenance, pregnancy, then growth and milk (milk first for a lactating cow); a shortfall is drawn from the reserve (at most 0.08 a month), a surplus stored in it (at most 0.07 a month, up to a ceiling). The reserve stays in the weight: frame and reserve together are the live weight, and a kg laid down costs more energy than a kg drawn gives back.

Silage and hay take about 0.5-1.5 months longer than before to finish, grass about the same; a Highland finishes on grass.

A dry cow holds her condition on hay and fattens on TMR (to BCS 4.0 at most). A thin cow (below BCS 2.2) stops giving her reserve to milk and gives less milk instead: on silage a Holstein gives about 82% of her milk (was 80%). A high-productivity cow eats up to 25% more (1 + 0.8 x (productivity - 1)) and gives more milk, but runs thinner. Suckler breeds (Angus, Limousin, Hereford, Highland) take more from forage: a grazing herd keeps BCS 2.2-2.7 on grass, and gets fat on TMR.

- Body condition: fixedFeedPrice in xml/bodyCondition.xml (default false, server only). The budget values a liter eaten at what an animal on full feed needs, so needing more made the same liters richer: the same 100 liters were worth 945.5 NU to a bull growing 10 kg a month and 1,345.5 NU to one growing 20, a steer's 15% faster growth was paid for by its feed, and a heavier or fatter body raised its own feed's value. With fixedFeedPrice a liter is worth what it is to an animal of the same genes on its full-feed line at its age and state, intact and healthy, whatever this one needs now; a disease's lost growth is spent on the disease instead of laid down as reserve (also when the disease stops all growth), and a cow's feed is priced for a healthy cow's milk while she would give milk without her disease. An animal ahead of its line (a steer's x1.15) gets the line's growth energy it cannot use as surplus, into the reserve up to the same ceiling as now; steers reach their slaughter reference about a month later than with the need-based price, which paid for the 15%. Bulls on TMR finish in the same month and the dairy cycles change by under 0.1 BCS, but animals behind their line get only maintenance-level feed once the line is grown: an Angus on hay takes 31.0 months to its slaughter reference instead of 29.4, and a Highland bull on grass stops at about 470 kg and never finishes (43.6 months before). It stays off until the growth curves are replaced.

- Weight model v2 for cattle (xml/weightProfiles.xml enabled="true", with the body condition on; scripts/animal/RLGrowth*.lua). Every cattle subtype whose breed resolves to one of the 22 physical breeds (map aliases included) grows on its breed's female or male curve: a monotone cubic Hermite through the design's knots (weight and gain continuous, no overshoot) from the mean birth weight to the adult plateau, flat after it. The seven Cow Breeds beef bulls' curves meet their confirmed admission weights exactly; the pack and map breeds' adults are the design's research candidates (a Charolais pack bull grows to 1,260 kg, not the pack's 1,050). An animal's target weight - what the slaughter reference (target x maturity), the finishing reserve and the finish verdict read - stays the subtype's configured target x its size gene, so a Charolais pack bull is still finished at 787.5 kg, where its curve passes the design's 790 kg at 20 months; the info box and the shop show that target weight, as before. The metabolism gene acts three bounded ways instead of twice on growth: adult weight x (1 + 0.12 z), development speed x (1 + 0.10 z), maintenance and the feed it asks for x (1 + 0.15 z), z = (m - 1) / 0.75 (a 1.75 bull: 1,120 kg adult and 115% of the food, not 1,300 kg and 175%). Each animal carries its actual birth weight, its adult weight, its reserve mass (kg) and its development (months along its curve), saved and streamed with it. The budget runs from birth every hour (an hour without a feed ledger takes the pen's food factor of its request, never an old weight rule; that hour's milk stays the old rule's in every store, and the budget pays for that milk): maintenance, pregnancy, then growth and milk; only the growth paid for develops the frame, so an underfed animal falls behind and, fed again, grows on from where it is - no jump to its age, no catch-up beyond its speed; a starving one breaks down frame and moves back on its curve. New frame is laid down at the animal's reserve index; surplus goes into the reserve. Births draw each calf around its own mean: breed and sex, a cross (mean of both breeds; buffalo never crosses), twins 85%, triplets 75%, a heifer's first calf 93%, the dam's reserve (0.90-1.03), the calf's gene (1 + 0.05 (m - 1)), normal CV 0.10 truncated at 2.5 SD; the survival roll keeps its old rule exactly (drawn first, as before), and the draw is the calf's weight - nothing lifts it to the mean later. Castration, calving, drying off, weaning, moves and loads change no weight: a steer's development eases to 0.90 of a bull's and a change of intake capacity takes about half a month. An old savegame's animal keeps its weight exactly: its body condition's reserve index (else the neutral 0.45) splits it into frame and reserve and its development is where its curve has that frame; one above its new adult weight is not slimmed. It grew on the old line, which at some ages is 20-30% above or below the new curve (a Highland bull at 24 months is at 78% of it, a buffalo cow at 116-124%, a 3-month Limousin bull at 131%), so its price, food request and underweight check keep a legacy anchor (new curve / old line at its age when taken over): on load all three are exactly the old rules', and the anchor fades out over migrationBlendMonths (12; the price moves at most about 2% a month). Its target weight (the old 1 + 0.4 (m - 1) to the size gene) and its food request's gene factor (m to 1 + 0.15 z) move the same way, and its underweight check starts as the old minWeight rule. One the change of model left behind its new curve catches up during those months (at most twice its speed, its budget paying), so it is not counted stunted once the anchor is gone; growth lost for want of feed never catches up. Hof Bergmann's MILKCOW (old fallback weights) starts at its old price and checks the same way, then is measured against the Holstein curve like the rest. A weight changed outside the model is split again on the next step. Live prices and food requests read an animal's share of its curve on the line they were calibrated on, so an animal on its curve costs and fetches what one on the old line did (within 1e-9, base and pack breeds); the underweight health penalty starts below 60% of its curve at its age (growth that fell behind does not catch up, so a calf starved for three months and fed again, at about 70%, is not penalised). Outside a rearing pen a calf's share of the trough stands for its whole diet, suckling included. In a rearing pen a calf's milk and solid liters are valued separately - solid feed at its energy x solidEnergy (calfRearing.xml, 0.40 at birth to 1 after weaning) - and the caps and that worth move hour by hour, with no step at weaning or 6 months. Hof Bergmann's MILKCOW, which has no weights and does not reproduce, is put on the Holstein female curve as a stand-in where the game holds it as individual animals (its milk read only, its milk store unchanged, its price the same share-of-curve rule as the rest); held as vanilla clusters it is left alone, and rlWeightProfiles reports which. On TMR every base bull reaches its slaughter reference within 0.4 months of its curve, finished (F72); forage takes longer and never stalls (Angus silage 19.8, hay 24.3 months; Highland on grass 27.2); the dairy and suckler cycles stay within 0.1 BCS of before. The sire's breed is recorded at conception (saved, streamed) for the cross. The hourly state event sends each stepped animal's v2 state to clients. fixedFeedPrice, when on, prices a liter by an average animal on its breed's curve at this one's age. All curve values, gene effects and birth factors are design candidates, not measured; with enabled="false" every animal keeps the old rules and the saved states are kept.
- Real-equivalent nutrition for cattle (off by default: RL settings > Nutrition model, Legacy / Real-equivalent (1/5), per savegame; needs the weight model v2 and the body condition on and xml/feedProfiles.xml valid). Intake and ration are worked out in real units - kg of dry matter (DM) a real day and ME in MJ, a game month standing for 30 model days - and only the feed taken from the trough is scaled: liters = resourceScale (0.20) x kg DM / (density x DM share), and the liters eaten restore DM the same way back, once on each side; weight, growth, health and milk do not change with the scale. Each feed fill type has a profile (feedProfiles.xml: density = the engine's massPerLiter, DM, ME from TDN by the NRC 2000 equation, crude protein, NDF and its effective share, starch, sugar, the feeding stages it may be fed in, source and status; the game's TMR counts as one standard TMR, never as its parts; Hof Bergmann's ROUNDBALE is a bale marker and left uneaten). Every hour a pen running the mode plans each animal's ration by DM share: milk for calves (up to a breed-scaled real milk allowance), the pen's forage in the map's order and, for weaned animals, concentrate - maize, barley, oats, wheat (registered as a grain group in every cattle trough after the map's food is loaded; under the old rules it is never eaten and the trough takes no new grain) and sugar beet - at a share the pen sets or, AUTO, the one that buys the most growth and milk; it is held where effective fibre, starch + sugar and wheat (30% of the ration) stay in bounds and to what the animal's rumen is adapted to (stepping up over 30 model days), and an empty forage trough never runs on pure grain (the grain stays). Intake is the least of appetite (per kg^0.75; a lactating cow's by her real milk), rumen fill (NDF 1.2% of weight) and, for GROW and MAINTAIN targets, energy demand (pen targets AUTO / FINISH / GROW / MAINTAIN). The engine's consumeFood is replaced in such pens by the plan; what the trough really lost is shared out and valued at each feed's own ME, so a shortage never makes the rest richer. The growth budget then runs on that ME (maintenance 0.5 MJ/kg^0.75 a day, frame growth 50 MJ/kg at 500 kg scaled by weight^0.75, reserve 52 in / 38 out), with protein below a stage's need limiting growth and milk; milk costs its real equivalent (each breed's real lactation average against its game curve). Health in these pens follows what the hour paid for, at per-month rates the same at any month length: up while feed covers maintenance and pregnancy, unchanged while the reserve or a short gap (3 model days) covers it, down by the share left to frame breakdown or unpaid, and for missing water or protein below maintenance; the old food-factor threshold and the 60%-of-curve penalty are replaced by a 45%-of-curve protection only, so a cow held on MAINTAIN keeps her weight and health for months. A pen runs the mode only as a whole: every animal covered by the weight model v2, and no calf still on milk without its lactating dam outside a calf rearing pen (SUCKLING_CALF); Hof Bergmann's MILKCOW keeps the old rules. A pen's ration settings and its feed months (liters, kg DM and MJ per feed, the scale and feed data they were counted under) are saved with its trough; an animal's rumen adaptation and built-up shortfall with its growth state; the feed data's fingerprint in rm_RlSettings.xml. Panels: the pen info box shows the mode (or why not), ration, energy fed and the feed that runs out first; an animal shows its real DM intake against plan, concentrate share and what limits it, and a bull its finishing forecast (months, or stalled: feed more); the RL menu lists one food row per feed (more than five when needed) with stock, DM share and planned liters a month, and the feed forecast is per feed. RLFeedRationEvent sets a pen's ration (server checked, every client told); the hourly state event carries the mode and each animal's feed for clients; RLPEN FEED_DAY logs each pen's day. Console: rlFeedReport [animals] [pen], rlFeedRation <pen> target= grain= share= substitute=. Offline, at s = 0.20 the game feed bulls eat to their slaughter reference is about the old rules' on TMR (x1.28) and silage, three times on hay (it is bulky); hay finishes slower, grass far faster and keeps its health, grain makes hay and grass routes fast. All coefficients are literature typical values or stated proxies, not measured herds; the mode stays off until accepted in game.

### Fixed

- Weight no longer depends on the days per period. The old weight rule (every animal outside the body condition budget: calves under 6 months, other species, cattle in an hour without a feed ledger) took an animal above its target weight back towards it, and a starving animal's weight down, by a share per game hour, so a month of 7 days lost seven times what a month of 1 day did: a 500 kg animal (target 1000) starving for a month kept 413.6 kg at 1 day per period, 193.2 kg at 3 and 0.001 kg at 7 or 28. Both losses are now integrated over the month at their old hourly rates at 1 day per period (scripts/animal/RLWeightMath.lua): at 1 day per period, the game's default, every hour is exactly as before (except past mature age + 75 months, below); any other period length gives the same month. Growth already was per period; an animal above its target that still grows follows the old hourly recurrence in closed form, so growth and the return to the target together give the same month at any period length too (apart from a few grams once, in the step in which a growing animal crosses its target).
- Healthy old animals no longer lose weight. The growth line's age factor, 1 + (mature age - age) / 75, turned negative 75 months after mature age and became a loss: a fed 25-year-old Holstein at her target lost 63 kg a month. With the configured mature ages it began at 93 months (7.8 years) for cows, 9.6-11.5 years for bulls, about 7 years for pigs, sheep, goats and chickens and 9 years for horses. With it went the old rule's inverted metabolism for such an animal (an overweight one returned to its target at 2 - metabolism instead of its own). The factor now stops at 0, in the old weight rule, the body condition budget (no more age loss) and the full-feed line the dealer's weights and live prices are read against (RLBreedTraits.getLineGain / getGrownLine). Up to mature age + 75 months every line is exactly the old one. The line is now computed in closed form instead of month by month up to the animal's age, which every live price used to loop through. Animals that already lost weight this way keep their weight (nothing is added back) and, being below their line, are priced as lighter animals; their price no longer follows the old falling line.
- An animal created without a weight (the dealer's stock, a new game's herds) is drawn at 85-115% of the full-feed line for its age; a newborn's draws below the subtype's minWeight were lifted to it, so half of them weighed exactly minWeight. minWeight is the line's birth weight, not a floor: a newborn keeps its whole spread.
